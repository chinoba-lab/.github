## What this is

`lab/` is the **Laboratory** of Chinoba — the layer where knowledge is still
being made. It is the counterpart to the **Library** at
[`docs/research/`](../docs/research/), which holds stable, settled knowledge.

> **Library** — knowledge that has settled.
> **Laboratory** — knowledge still in motion.

The Library publishes conclusions. The Laboratory publishes the *process* that
produces them: the open questions, the discussions, the drafts, the reviews, the
experiments — including the ones that did not work. Together they make Chinoba a
place where you can see not only *what* is known, but *how it came to be known*.

→ Why the Laboratory is open: [`../OPEN_RESEARCH.md`](../OPEN_RESEARCH.md)

## The research workflow

Every idea in Chinoba travels the same path. Each stage produces an artifact,
and each stage is open to community feedback.

```
        Idea
          ↓
      Discussion            ← lab/discussions/
          ↓
    Research Note           ← lab/notes/
          ↓
 Architecture Proposal      ← lab/proposals/
          ↓
  Architecture Review       ← lab/reviews/ · lab/architectures/
          ↓
     Experiment             ← lab/experiments/
          ↓
      Revision              ← the loop back to any earlier stage
          ↓
        Paper               ← lab/papers/
          ↓
        Book                ← promoted toward the Library
          ↓
   Public Lecture           ← lab/talks/
```

No stage is the finish line. An experiment can send an idea back to discussion;
a review can reopen a research note. Knowledge here is a **continuous process**,
and the workflow is a cycle, not a ladder.

## From Laboratory to Library

An idea does not stay in the Laboratory forever. When it has been discussed,
designed, reviewed, and tested — when it has *settled* — it can be **promoted**
into the Library at [`docs/research/`](../docs/research/).

Promotion is a decision, made the way Chinoba makes every decision: a signal
(the mature artifact), a review against the standards, a human who says yes, and
a trace that records why. The Laboratory record is not deleted when an idea
graduates; the discussion and experiments that produced it remain, so the
evolution stays legible.

> A finished document is only one stage of knowledge. The Laboratory keeps the
> rest.

## How to take part

Anyone can contribute at any stage — you do not have to write code to move an
idea forward.

- Open a **discussion** with a question.
- Turn a discussion into a **research note**.
- Shape a note into a **proposal**.
- **Review** a proposal or an architecture.
- Propose or run an **experiment**.
- Suggest a **reference** or a related idea.
