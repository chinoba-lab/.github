## What this is

A paper is a stage on the path of an idea — the point where discussions, notes,
architectures, and experiments are drawn together into a single, careful
argument meant to be read beyond the laboratory.

> A finished document is only one stage of knowledge. A paper is a snapshot of
> what is understood *so far* — clear, cited, and open to challenge.

Papers here are written and reviewed in the open. A draft paper is expected to
change in response to [review](../reviews/), and its earlier stages remain linked
so a reader can trace the argument back to the questions that produced it.

---

## What a paper carries

A paper draws its material from the stages before it:

- the **question** from [discussions](../discussions/);
- the **reasoning** from [research notes](../notes/);
- the **design** from [architectures](../architectures/);
- the **evidence** from [experiments](../experiments/).

Its claims should be traceable to those sources. Nothing in a paper should
appear without a trail back to where it came from.

---

## Paper discussion and review

A paper is opened for **paper discussion** (see [discussions](../discussions/))
and **review** (see [reviews](../reviews/)) before it is considered settled. The
dialogue is part of the paper's record.

---

## From paper to book to lecture

A paper is not the end of the path:

```
Paper → Book → Public Lecture
```

Papers accumulate into longer-form knowledge (a **book**), and settled knowledge
is prepared to be spoken (a **lecture**, in [`../talks/`](../talks/)). Along the
way, mature findings can be **promoted** into the Library at
[`../../docs/research/`](../../docs/research/).
