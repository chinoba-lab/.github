## What this is

This room documents Chinoba's **collaborations** — the research the laboratory
does together with others. Because intelligence is relational, the laboratory's
most important relationships are often the ones that cross its own boundary: with
universities, companies, communities, open-source projects, and research groups.

> Knowledge grows through relationships. A collaboration is a relationship the
> laboratory chooses to keep in the open, so far as it can.

---

## Who we collaborate with

| Partner | Typical collaboration |
|---|---|
| **Universities** | Joint research, student projects, academic review |
| **Companies** | Applied research, real-world problems, shared tooling |
| **Communities** | Open discussion, shared standards, mutual learning |
| **Open Source projects** | Shared code, interoperability, upstream contribution |
| **Research groups** | Co-authored work, cross-lab experiments |

---

## Open by Default, Private when Necessary

Collaboration is where the laboratory's two privacy principles meet most
directly. Much of a collaboration can be shared — its purpose, its public
outcomes, the ideas it produced. Some of it cannot: a partner's unpublished work,
a confidence given in trust, personal data.

Collaborations are documented using
[`../../templates/collaboration-template.md`](../../templates/collaboration-template.md),
which separates **Public Notes** from **Private Notes**. The private notes are
*not stored* in this repository — the template records only *that a private layer
exists*, so the trace stays honest without exposing what was shared in
confidence.

---

## What a collaboration record contains

- **Organization** and **Contact** — who we are working with.
- **Purpose** — why we are working together.
- **Discussion Summary** and **Shared Ideas** — what the collaboration produced.
- **Next Steps** — where it is heading.
- **Public Notes** — everything that can be shared.
- **Private Notes (not stored)** — a marker that a private layer exists.

---

## Collaborations feed the laboratory

A collaboration is rarely an endpoint. The ideas it produces flow into
[discussions](../discussions/), [research notes](../notes/), and
[experiments](../experiments/) — the same workflow as any other idea, now shaped
by more than one group.
