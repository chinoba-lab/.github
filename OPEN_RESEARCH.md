## Why Chinoba Lab is open

Chinoba's central claim is that **intelligence is relational** — it does not
live inside a single mind or a single model, but in the relationships between
signals, decisions, and the people and systems that exchange them. A research
laboratory built on that claim cannot be closed. If intelligence is what happens
*between* participants, then the research itself must happen in the open, where
those relationships can form.

So Chinoba Lab is not merely an open-source repository that publishes finished
results. It is an **open research laboratory**: a place where the *process* of
thinking — the questions, the discussions, the half-formed architectures, the
experiments that failed — is visible as it unfolds.

> We believe knowledge becomes more valuable when ideas, discussions,
> architecture, experiments, and feedback are shared openly.

## The principles

Eight principles govern how research is conducted here. They are not rules so
much as a stance toward knowledge.

- **Open by Default** — Every idea, discussion, and experiment is public unless
  there is a specific reason it cannot be.
- **Private when Necessary** — Some things — personal data, a collaborator's
  unpublished work, a confidence given in trust — are held back deliberately.
  Privacy here is a considered exception, never the default.
- **Communication creates Intelligence** — Intelligence is not stored; it is
  produced in the exchange. The conversation is not overhead on the research —
  it *is* the research.
- **Knowledge evolves through discussion** — A claim gets sharper when it is
  questioned. Discussion is the mechanism of refinement, not a formality after
  the fact.
- **Ideas mature through collaboration** — The best version of an idea is rarely
  the first author's version. Ideas ripen when more relationships touch them.
- **Architecture evolves through review** — Designs are proposed, reviewed, and
  revised in public. A design's history is part of its meaning.
- **Research is a continuous process** — There is no final state. Knowledge is
  always somewhere along a path from question to lecture, and always able to
  move again.
- **Finished documents are only one stage of knowledge** — A polished paper is a
  snapshot, not the whole. The discussions and experiments behind it are
  first-class knowledge too.

## Transparent Knowledge Evolution

Most repositories publish conclusions. Chinoba Lab publishes the *evolution* of a
conclusion — and keeps that evolution legible after the fact.

This mirrors Chinoba's own **Decision Trace Model**: a decision is not a single
output but a *traceable transformation* from signal to action. Applied to
research, the trace looks like this —

```
Idea → Discussion → Research Note → Architecture → Experiment → Paper → Book → Lecture
```

Every stage leaves a record. Anyone arriving later can replay how an idea became
knowledge, question any step, and re-open it. The history is not archived away;
it is part of the knowledge itself.

## Research as Dialogue

A monologue states. A dialogue discovers. Chinoba Lab treats research as an
ongoing dialogue — between authors and reviewers, between disciplines, between
the laboratory and the communities, universities, and companies it works with.

This is why the laboratory keeps a dedicated space for open questions and
discussion before anything is concluded (see [`lab/discussions/`](lab/discussions/)).
The question comes first; the answer is negotiated in public.

---

## Knowledge grows through relationships

No idea in this laboratory stands alone. Every research note links to the
discussions that shaped it; every architecture links to the reviews that
challenged it; every collaboration links to the shared ideas it produced. The
value of the laboratory is not the number of documents it holds but the density
of the relationships between them.

> Intelligence is relational. So is knowledge. So is this laboratory.

## Open by Default, Private when Necessary — in practice

| Kind of content | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ideas, questions, discussions | **Public** | The raw material of the lab |
| Research notes and architectures | **Public** | Including drafts and revisions |
| Experiments and their results | **Public** | Including negative results |
| Reviews and feedback | **Public** | The dialogue is the value |
| Personal or third-party private data | **Private** | Never committed |
| A collaborator's unpublished work | **Private** | Shared only with consent |
| Confidences given in trust | **Private** | Recorded as "not stored" |

When something must stay private, the laboratory records *that a private layer
exists* without recording its contents — so the trace remains honest even where
it is incomplete.

## Library and Laboratory

Chinoba Lab is two things at once:

- a **Library** — [`docs/research/`](docs/research/) — stable, settled knowledge;
- a **Laboratory** — [`lab/`](lab/) — knowledge still being made.

Ideas begin in the Laboratory and, once they mature, are **promoted** into the
Library. Nothing here removes or rewrites the Library; the Laboratory only adds
the layer where ideas are still moving.
