`chinoba-lab` is **the Commons** — the surface where Chinoba's research becomes
public, supported, open-source software. It is not a company brand and not a
personal portfolio; it is the *stewarding body* for the ecosystem described in
the existing Chinoba documentation.

> The personal account answers "what is Masao building?" The organization
> answers "what is **Chinoba**, and can I depend on it?"

---

## Why an organization, and why now

The existing architecture grew up entirely under one personal account. That was
right for a single researcher's exploration — but the ecosystem has crossed a
threshold:

- there are now **multiple official, interdependent repositories** that form a
  coherent system, not a set of personal experiments;
- the project has a **public identity** (`chinoba.org`) that people and
  organizations are beginning to depend on;
- contribution, governance, and accountability now need to belong to a **named
  entity**, not an individual's namespace.

An organization gives Chinoba what the research itself argues for: **trust you
can establish, trace, and verify**. A repo under `chinoba-lab` carries an
institutional promise that a repo under a personal handle cannot.

## Naming: "Lab", deliberately

The suffix **"Lab"** is a promise about *posture*, not size. Chinoba is research
infrastructure: things are studied, measured, and improved here. "Lab" signals
that the Commons stays close to inquiry even as it ships supported software —
and it keeps a clean conceptual line to the personal **Bench**, where the
rawer experiments happen.

- **Bench** (personal) — where you try things.
- **Lab** (org) — where things become reliable.
